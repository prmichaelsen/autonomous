### **Real-World Research: Laser Blinding Attack**

**University of Florida Study (2022):**
- Published at 2023 USENIX Security Symposium
- Researchers from UF, University of Michigan, and University of Electro-Communications (Japan)
- **FIRST TIME lidar sensors were successfully tricked into deleting obstacle data**
- [Publicly available research](https://cpseclab.github.io/youcantseeme/)

**How the Attack Works:**

1. **Laser Timing**: Attacker uses precisely timed laser pulses synchronized to the LiDAR sensor
2. **Fake Reflections**: Laser creates false reflections that mimic genuine LiDAR returns
3. **Data Deletion**: LiDAR sensor automatically discards REAL obstacle data, keeping only the fake reflections
4. **Blind Spot Created**: Large blind spot forms in front of vehicle - pedestrians and obstacles completely invisible
5. **Dangerous Behavior**: Vehicle thinks road is clear, continues accelerating toward hidden obstacles

**Demonstration Results:**
- ✅ Successfully deleted data for static obstacles
- ✅ Successfully deleted moving pedestrians from view
- ✅ Worked on moving vehicles and robots
- ✅ Attack positioned 15 feet away from roadside
- ✅ Could work from farther away with upgraded equipment
- ✅ Used "fairly basic" technology
- ✅ Followed slow-moving vehicle using basic camera tracking

**Technical Requirements:**
- Laser must be **perfectly timed** to LiDAR sensor
- Moving vehicles must be **carefully tracked** to keep laser pointed correctly
- Synchronization information usually **publicly available from manufacturer**
- "Primarily a matter of synchronization" - S. Hrushikesh Bhupathiraj (UF doctoral student)

### **LiDAR Technology Specs**

**Automotive LiDAR Wavelengths:**
- **905nm (near-infrared)**: Most common, cost-effective, semiconductor laser
- **1550nm (infrared)**: Better eye safety, longer range, fiber laser
- **532nm (green)**: Used in bathymetric (underwater) LiDAR, NOT typical for automotive

**Key Insight:** Most autonomous vehicles use **905nm or 1550nm LiDAR**, NOT green lasers. However:
- Green laser (532nm) can still create interference through timing attacks
- Attack works by mimicking reflections, not matching wavelength exactly
- Attacker needs laser synchronized to victim's LiDAR pulse rate

### **Green Laser Specifications**

**532nm Green Laser:**
- Common in laser pointers and tactical applications
- DPSS (Diode-Pumped Solid State) technology
- Generates green light by frequency-doubling 1064nm infrared
- Power range: 5mW (pointer) to 1000mW+ (high-power tactical)
- Pulse rate: Up to 60,000 pulses per second (bathymetric LiDAR)
- Highly visible to human eye

**Why Green Laser for Attack:**
- ✅ Visible beam makes aiming easier
- ✅ High power models readily available
- ✅ Can be synchronized to target LiDAR
- ✅ Creates dramatic visual effect for film
- ⚠️ Not the "natural" wavelength for automotive LiDAR, but attack still works
